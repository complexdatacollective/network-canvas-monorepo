<Definition>
  Forms allow you to assign variables to ego, nodes, or edges using a familiar
  and easy-to-use interface.
</Definition>

## Details

Forms are one of the primary ways you will assign values to your [variables](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/variables) in Interviewer. They work by associating each **variable** with an **[input control](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/input-controls)**, a **question prompt** and **[validation](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/field-validation) options**, to form a **field**. Each form can have as many fields as you wish, in any order.

![](/assets/img/key-concepts/forms/preview.png)

When using Architect to design your protocol you will encounter several places where you can build a form - particularly when creating stages that use [Interfaces](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/interfaces) such as the Name Generator, the Per Alter Form, and the Ego Form.

## Constructing a form

Forms are comprised of one or more fields, which can be reordered to appear in any sequence. The sequence they appear in Architect is the same sequence they will be shown in Interviewer. To begin creating a form, locate the form section within the Interface you are configuring.

![](/assets/img/key-concepts/forms/form.png)

<AppOnly app="current">

A form is written as one piece of text, in the **Form content** editor of the stage's **Form** section. The text is what participants read, from top to bottom: headings, paragraphs, lists, images and the form's fields. Each field is a block in the text, placed where its question is asked. A field shows its question, its hint and its input control as participants will see them, so the editor shows what the form will look like.

### Adding a field

1. Place the cursor where the field should go.
2. Choose **Field** in the editor's toolbar, or press Ctrl+Shift+F (⌘+Shift+F on a Mac).
3. The new field appears in the text with a dashed outline, and a list of attributes opens at the **Field** button, or beside the new field when you use the keyboard shortcut. Choose the attribute to which the answers are saved. To create a new attribute, type its name in the search box, choose **Create “…”**, and then choose the type of answer that it records.
4. Type the question where the field reads **Type the question**. If participants need more guidance, type a hint where it reads **Add a hint (optional)**.

The list contains the attributes of the participant, node or edge that the form asks about. An attribute that cannot be used in the field stays in the list, dimmed, with the reason beneath it. For example, each attribute can be collected by only one field of a form, so an attribute that another field already collects cannot be chosen.

If you close the list without choosing an attribute, a field that is still empty is removed. A field that already has a question or a hint stays in the text, and shows **Choose an attribute** in place of its attribute: choose it to open the list again. The stage cannot be saved until every field has an attribute.

The question and the hint are written in the field itself. They can be bold or italic, and they can show [piped text](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/piped-text), such as the label of the node that the form asks about.

### The settings of a field

To select a field, click its outline or its input control. From the keyboard, press Escape while the cursor is in its question or hint. The settings of a selected field open beside it:

- **Attribute**: the attribute to which the answers are saved.
- **Input control**: how the participant answers the question. The list contains the input controls for the attribute's type, which was chosen when the attribute was created.
- **Options**: the answers that the participant can choose from, for an attribute that has them. **Edit options** opens a dialog in which you change them. For an input control that has settings of its own, such as the labels at the ends of a scale, the button reads **Edit control settings**.
- **Validation**: the attribute's [validation rules](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/field-validation).
- **Show validation hints**, which shows participants, before they answer, the rules that their answer must follow, for example "Must choose at least 1 option."
- A note saying that the input control, options and validation are part of the attribute, so changes made here apply to every form that uses it. The note also names any other stage that uses the attribute.
- **Delete field**, which removes the field from the form.

Changes to the attribute are saved with the stage, when you save it.

### Arranging the form

To move a field, drag it by its handle, or select it and press Ctrl+Shift+Up arrow or Ctrl+Shift+Down arrow (⌘+Shift on a Mac). Text, headings, images, audio and video can come before, between and after the fields: for example, a heading at the top of the form and a paragraph that introduces a group of questions. A field cannot be placed inside a list.

Every form must contain at least one field. Otherwise, Architect reports **Add at least one field to this form.**

### Showing fields only in some cases

A form can contain [logic blocks](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/piped-text#logic-blocks). A logic block shows different content in different cases: for example, one set of questions for participants who are students, and another set for participants who are not. Choose **Logic block** in the toolbar, choose its logic, and then add text and fields to each of its versions. During the interview, the participant sees only the content of the version that applies, and the form changes as the answers that the logic depends on change.

When the participant submits the form, the answers to any fields that a logic block hides are cleared. This is the case even when the participant answered a field before it was hidden.

### Forms in more than one language

When a protocol has [more than one language](/en/design-protocols/tutorials/translating-your-protocol), each language has its own form text. The forms in different languages can be laid out differently, and they can even contain different fields. A field that is in the forms of two languages is the same field: it saves its answers to the same attribute and has the same settings, and only its question and hint are translated.

To write the form in another language, choose that language in the editor's language menu. When you add a field there and choose an attribute that a field in another language already collects, Architect adds that same field.

When the form in the language that you are editing contains different fields from the form in the default language, a note above the editor names them. For example: "The Spanish form does not include 1 field that the English form contains: employer. Forms in different languages can contain different fields." The note is for information only. However, the form in every language must contain at least one field.

The text of a form can also be edited in the [translation table](/en/design-protocols/tutorials/translating-your-protocol#editing-translations), where the question and the hint of each field have rows of their own. There, a field can be added to the text of one language only if the form already has it in another language, and a field cannot be removed from the form in all languages at once. New fields are added, and fields are removed from the whole form, in the stage editor.

### Name Generator and Network Composer forms

A Name Generator's form also has a **Dialog title**, which participants see as the heading of the dialog in which they add a person.

The forms of a [Network Composer](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/network-composer) stage are not written in this way. Their fields are set up in the stage editor, as described under [Attribute forms](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/network-composer#attribute-forms) on that page.

</AppOnly>

Set the form heading, and then create fields for each of the variables you wish to capture on this form by clicking the **create new** button.

When creating a new field, you will first either create a new variable or select an existing one from the variable chooser. Next, you will type the question [prompt](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/prompts) to be shown next to the input control. The prompt supports markdown formatting.

You can also provide an optional **hint** for the field. The hint is markdown-formatted guidance displayed below the question to help participants understand how to answer (for example, "Select all that apply..."). Leave it blank if no extra guidance is needed.

Next, you must decide which [input control](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/input-controls) to use. For new variables, this will determine the variable type.

<TipBox danger>

Once you set a variable type (by choosing its input type when first used), you cannot change it later. You can still adjust the specific input control on your form after the type is set, as long as the input control is compatible with the variable type.

</TipBox>

</AppOnly>

After setting the input control, set any variable-specific options and configure the [validation options](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/field-validation) you require. Validation helps ensure data quality by enforcing rules like required fields, format constraints, and value ranges. You can also enable **Show validation hints** for the field, which automatically displays hints derived from the field's validation rules, helping participants understand the input requirements without you having to write them out by hand.

## Considerations

When designing forms, keep these points in mind:

- Although it may be tempting to create long and elaborate forms, as is typical within other more traditional survey software, consider the burden of overloaded forms on participants and data quality. For example, while you can capture categorical data within a form, you might elect to use the Categorical Bin Interface instead. Network Canvas Interviewer is not intended to replace existing survey methods.
- Making strategic use of forms allows you to capture attribute data systematically. However, you lose the interactive qualities (e.g. dragging and dropping) of other Interfaces that may improve the interview experience for participants and potentially render higher quality data.
- If you use the same variable in multiple places in your interview (for example in different forms), any changes you make to the input control or validation options will apply to _all_ uses of the variable.

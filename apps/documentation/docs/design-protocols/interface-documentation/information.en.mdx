The Information Interface is a general purpose screen that can be used to present information to participants using a variety of text and media resources. For example, this screen can be useful to introduce a study or to provide instructions or context for a particular stage in the survey.

A page can show images, videos and audio recordings alongside its text. These are [resources](/en/design-protocols/key-concepts/resources), chosen from your resource library in Architect or added from your device. There is no limit on how much a page can contain: participants can scroll through the screen, so add as much as you need.

## Configuring Information Screen

An information screen is one piece of text, written in a single editor: the **Rich Text** field in the stage's **Content** section. Everything that the page shows is part of that text, in the order in which participants read it: headings, paragraphs, lists, and any images, videos or audio recordings. The editor grows as you write, so the whole page stays in view. A stage whose page is empty cannot be saved.

To give the page a heading, place the cursor in the first line of the text and choose **Heading 1** from the list of text styles in the editor's toolbar. The list shows each style as it looks in the text, so you can see how large each heading is. A heading at the start of the page is centered, in the same way as the page heading was in earlier versions of Architect.

### Adding images, video and audio

To add a file, place the cursor where it should go and choose **Insert image**, **Insert audio** or **Insert video** in the editor's toolbar, then pick the resource. The file is added as a block of its own between the paragraphs around it. A block can be dragged to another place in the text, and removed like any other part of it. When you select text that runs across a block, the block is shaded as part of the selection, and is copied, cut or deleted with the text.

Select a block to change how it is shown. It is outlined, and its settings open next to it and stay open while the block is selected. From the keyboard, press Enter on a selected block to move into its settings and Escape to return to the text. In a small window the settings scroll, so you can reach all of them.

- **Description** is read aloud to participants who use screen reader technology. Leave it blank for an image that is only decorative.
- **Display size** applies to images and videos. **Full size** shows them at their natural height, while **Small**, **Medium** and **Large** limit their height and keep their proportions. You can also drag the round handle at the bottom corner of a selected image or video: drag it up or inwards to show the image or video smaller, and down or outwards to show it larger. A label shows the size that the image or video will have, and that size is applied when you release the handle.
- **Alignment** places an audio player, or an image or video whose **Display size** is not **Full size**, where a line of text starts, in the center, or where a line ends.
- **Start playing automatically** applies to audio and video. When it is on, the recording starts as the participant reaches the page. A video that starts automatically is muted until the participant turns its sound on, because browsers do not let a page start a video with sound.
- **Repeat when it ends** applies to audio and video, and starts the recording again each time it finishes.
- **Image**, **Audio** or **Video** shows the file that the block contains. Choose **Change the image**, **Change the audio file** or **Change the video** to pick another resource in its place. The block keeps its description, size, alignment and playback settings.
- **Delete** takes the block out of the text. The file stays in your resource library.

When a protocol has [more than one language](/en/design-protocols/tutorials/translating-your-protocol), each language has its own text, with its own blocks. This lets a page show a different image or recording in each language, and means that a file added in one language is not shown in another language until you add it there too.

### Best Practices

<GoodPractice>

Use this Interface to explain stage activities to participants to increase comprehension and engagement. This may be especially important for increasing survey accessibility.

</GoodPractice>

### Try to Avoid

<BadPractice>

Resist overloading the Interface with text and instead consider using other
resource types to communicate information to your participants.

</BadPractice>

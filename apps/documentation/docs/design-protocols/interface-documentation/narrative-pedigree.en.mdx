The Narrative Pedigree is a companion to the [Family Pedigree](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/family-pedigree). It takes a family pedigree the participant has already built and draws it using standard pedigree notation, overlaying inheritance information for one or more conditions. It does not create or change any data — it only reads and displays the pedigree from its source stage.

Use it to show, at a glance, who in the family is affected by a condition, who must carry it, and (optionally) who may be at risk — with the drawing rules following the standard clinical conventions described in Bennett et al. (2022).

## Choosing the source stage

The Narrative Pedigree always reads from an earlier **Family Pedigree** stage in the same protocol. Select that stage as the source. Only Family Pedigree stages that come before this one are offered.

The Narrative Pedigree draws the participant and everyone connected to them through the source stage's relationships: partners (current or not), parents of every kind, children, and their relatives in turn. Someone of the same person type who is not connected to the participant through those relationships is left out, even if another stage added them or tied them to the participant with a different kind of connection.

While a Narrative Pedigree reads a Family Pedigree, Architect will not let you change that Family Pedigree's person type, because every condition the Narrative Pedigree draws is an attribute of that type. Point the Narrative Pedigree at another source, or remove it, first.

<TipBox>

Place the Narrative Pedigree **after** the Family Pedigree that feeds it. Who has each condition is collected by that stage's [nomination prompts](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/family-pedigree#nomination-prompts), which participants answer once their family is drawn.

</TipBox>

## Configuring conditions

Add one or more conditions to display. For each condition you set:

- **Label** — the condition's name, shown in the key.
- **Color** — the color used to draw that condition's status on the pedigree.
- **Node attribute** — the **boolean** attribute that records whether each person is affected. Only attributes recorded by one of the source Family Pedigree's nomination prompts can be chosen, and each can be used by only one condition. If the list is empty, add a nomination prompt to the Family Pedigree asking who has the condition.
- **Inheritance pattern** — how the condition is passed down. This drives every inference the interface makes, so it must be correct.

Supported inheritance patterns are: autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked dominant, X-linked recessive, Y-linked, mitochondrial, multifactorial, and unknown. For **multifactorial** and **unknown**, only the people explicitly nominated as affected are shown — no carrier or at-risk inference is made.

## What the participant sees

The family is drawn as the Family Pedigree laid it out, on the same canvas: the participant can drag to pan, zoom with the mouse wheel, by pinching, with the + and − keys, or with the zoom buttons, and use **Show the whole family** to fit everyone on screen. A key beside the family lists the conditions and explains the symbols.

Each person is labelled as they were on the Family Pedigree. The participant is labelled "You", and everyone else by their name. A relative who was not named is labelled by how they are related to the participant, in the same words the Family Pedigree used ("Mother" or "Egg parent", for example). If your study encrypts the name attribute, the participant is asked for their passphrase, and relatives are labelled by how they are related until it is entered. A person recorded as adopted is drawn between brackets.

## How statuses are shown

Each person's symbol takes the shape their person type gives them in the codebook, as on the Family Pedigree. The Family Pedigree's [Symbols choice](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/family-pedigree#how-each-persons-symbol-is-drawn) maps sex assigned at birth to the standard shapes: a square for male, a circle for female, and a diamond for everyone else. With no condition selected, each person is drawn as a plain symbol. Selecting a condition in the key draws each person's **status** for that condition on their symbol, in the condition's color.

<KeyConcept title="Certain vs. possible statuses">

The interface distinguishes what is **certain** from the family structure (a person is affected, or must carry or develop the condition) from what is only **possible** (a person _may_ carry or develop it). Certain statuses are always shown. Possible ("at-risk") statuses are only shown when you turn them on — see below.

</KeyConcept>

Certain statuses include _affected_, _will develop it_ (an obligate/presymptomatic case), and _carrier_. These are drawn using the standard nomenclature symbols and explained in the on-screen key.

## Possible (at-risk) statuses

There is a **Show possible (at-risk) statuses** option, which is **off by default**. When it is off, the pedigree shows only certain statuses. When it is on, it also shows _may develop_ and _may carry_ — drawn as the usual symbol with a question mark added.

At-risk statuses are **inferred, not observed**. They are a strong visual signal that can be misread as established fact, so they are intended for **clinician-directed use**, where a professional interprets them in context. Standard pedigree nomenclature deliberately does not encode probabilistic risk.

<TipBox danger>

Anything the Narrative Pedigree marks as at-risk is inferred from the participant's own account, and is not a diagnosis. Turn that option on only when a clinician is interpreting the results, and never present it to a participant as a diagnosis.

</TipBox>

## How inheritance is inferred

When at-risk statuses are shown, the interface works out possible statuses from the family structure and each condition's inheritance pattern. A few rules are worth knowing, because they shape what researchers and clinicians will see:

- **Only biological connections transmit.** Only _biological_ and _donor_ relationships pass a condition on. Social, adoptive, surrogate, and partner relationships never do, so risk is never inferred across them.
- **Sex assigned at birth decides sex-linked inheritance.** X-linked and Y-linked inheritance depend on it, and gender identity plays no part. A person recorded as intersex is always treated as uncertain, because neither one X chromosome nor two can be assumed. A person whose sex is not known, who preferred not to say, or who was not asked is placed by the gamete they gave a child — the egg or the sperm — when the Family Pedigree's [gamete rule](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/family-pedigree#which-parent-gave-the-egg-or-the-sperm) works it out from the child's other parent, and is otherwise treated as uncertain. The interface leaves inheritance through an uncertain person unresolved rather than guessing.
- **X-linked risk follows the maternal line.** For X-linked recessive conditions, risk travels down the mother's X chromosome. Relatives connected only through an unaffected father — for example, a mother's half-sibling who shares only her father — carry none of that X and are correctly **not** marked at risk.
- **Mitochondrial inheritance follows the egg.** A condition carried in mitochondrial DNA is traced down the line of whoever gave the egg, as the Family Pedigree's [gamete rule](/en/design-protocols/interface-documentation/family-pedigree#which-parent-gave-the-egg-or-the-sperm) works it out from sex assigned at birth. Where a child was conceived with a donor egg, it follows the donor, not the person who carried the pregnancy. Mitochondrial donation, where one egg gives the nucleus and another the mitochondria, cannot be drawn in the Family Pedigree, which allows a child one genetic parent recorded female at birth; the Narrative Pedigree traces it correctly only in imported data that records two.

<KeyConcept title="A filled symbol always means affected">

A solid, filled symbol always means the person is **affected** — it is never used for someone who is only _at risk_, however that risk arises. This follows standard pedigree nomenclature (Bennett et al.), which reserves shading for people who actually have the condition and never encodes a probability in the shape itself.

So a relative who might have inherited a recessive condition from two carrier parents is shown with an ordinary at-risk symbol — _may develop_ where both parents are established carriers (a genuine one-in-four chance), or _may carry_ where their own carrier status is itself only possible — never a filled, affected-looking symbol. Reserving the fill for affected people keeps the strongest visual signal honest: it only ever marks a condition someone actually has.

</KeyConcept>

Throughout, the interface assumes conditions are fully penetrant (everyone who has the genotype shows the condition) and treats inference as a guide to _where to look_, not a substitute for clinical assessment.

## Focusing on one person

Selecting a condition from the key switches the pedigree to that condition's view. From there, selecting a person focuses on them: the relatives who contribute to _their_ inheritance of the selected condition stay highlighted and everyone else is dimmed, following the true source line for that pattern (for example, up the maternal line for an X-linked condition). People cannot be focused on until a condition is selected. **Clear focus**, the Escape key, or a click on an empty part of the canvas clears the focus.

The family can be used entirely from the keyboard. It is a single stop in the tab order; the arrow keys move between people by where they sit in the tree, and Enter or Space focuses on the person who has keyboard focus. Each person's status for the shown conditions is read out by screen readers, as is each change of condition or focus.

**Save snapshot** saves a picture of the whole family on a white background, as it is drawn for the selected condition and focus, with the symbol key once a condition is selected. It is titled with the stage's label, the condition, and the person focused on.

## Best practices

<GoodPractice>

Record who is affected with one Family Pedigree nomination prompt per condition, and set each condition's inheritance pattern carefully — every inference depends on it.

</GoodPractice>

<GoodPractice>

Capture **sex assigned at birth** for each person in the Family Pedigree. Sex-linked conditions cannot be traced without it, and missing sex is treated as uncertainty.

</GoodPractice>

## Try to avoid

<BadPractice>

Don't enable at-risk statuses for participant-facing use without a clinician. Inferred "may develop" or "may carry" symbols can be mistaken for a diagnosis.

</BadPractice>

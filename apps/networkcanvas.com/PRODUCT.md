# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Network researchers and research teams who need to design, run, and evaluate personal-network studies. Participants are a secondary audience when they complete interviews built with Network Canvas.

## Product Purpose

Network Canvas provides free, open-source tools for designing interview protocols, collecting complex network data, and moving from study design to usable research data with less specialist software expertise.

## Positioning

The project combines a visual protocol-authoring workflow with participant-centred network interview interfaces and deployment options for both interviewer-assisted and remote research.

## Operating Context

Researchers create and validate protocols in Architect, collect data in Interviewer or Fresco, consult task-focused documentation, and reuse published protocols as working examples. Current browser apps use Schema 8; Classic apps remain available for Schema 7 studies in maintenance mode.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The website is a statically exported, localized Next.js site with `en-US`, `en-GB`, and `es` routes.
- Research content and downloadable artifacts must be evidence-backed and must not rely on unsupported claims.
- Protocol migrations from Schema 7 to Schema 8 are one-way, so guidance must distinguish current and Classic applications accurately.
- Gallery protocol and codebook downloads are versioned static files shipped with the website.

## Brand Commitments

Use the Network Canvas name, established project imagery, plain research-focused language, and the shared Fresco component system. Preserve the project’s free and open-source GPLv3 positioning.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains localized site copy, project and research content CSVs, real product screenshots and illustrations, and a curated Protocol Gallery dataset with downloadable protocols, codebook summaries, publications, grants, populations, methods, contacts, and study descriptions.

## Product Principles

- Make complex network research workflows understandable without flattening their substance.
- Prefer real protocols, research evidence, and project artifacts over generic demonstrations.
- Keep researchers oriented to the right tool, schema version, and next action.
- Treat accessibility, localization, and participant burden as product requirements.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Interactive surfaces must be keyboard operable, expose state changes to assistive technology, respect reduced-motion preferences, preserve legibility under text expansion, and avoid relying on colour alone.

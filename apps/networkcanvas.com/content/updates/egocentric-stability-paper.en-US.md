# Paper in Network Science: how stable are personal networks over time?

When people are interviewed about their networks more than once, many of the people they name drop out and reappear between interviews. This paper asks whether that change reflects real churn in people's lives, or the interview itself: respondents anchoring on what a network should look like, or naming fewer people to shorten the interview.

Using three waves of a Chicago panel study of young men who have sex with men, collected with Network Canvas's touchscreen sociogram, the authors found little change in average network size across waves. Small but significant changes appeared between the first and second waves, mostly among people with the largest networks, and almost none between the second and third in network size, composition or density. [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/nws.2019.27).

# French (fr) terminology glossary

How Network Canvas names its concepts and interfaces in French. It applies to
every surface with French text: the apps’ and packages’ catalogs,
networkcanvas.com (its messages and its Updates pages), the documentation and
protocol text. It follows the conventions of France. Translate with it, and
review translations against it. The terms come from the French the apps
already ship, so a page names a screen, button or setting the way the reader
sees it in the app. Where a term is a social-network-analysis term, the
French sociology of networks decided it (see the Notes column).

When a translation needs a term that is not here, look for it in the app
catalogs first (`apps/*/src/locales/fr.json`, `packages/*/src/locales/fr.json`,
`packages/interview/src/locales/wording/fr.json`), then add it here in the
same change. A new or changed entry is verified like any translation (see the
`verifying-translations` skill), and catalog strings that use an old term
change with it.

## Register and style

- **Address the reader as vous**, as every catalog and the website do. Never
  "tu". Prefer impersonal or infinitive phrasing where it reads naturally
  ("Il est possible de…", "Pour ajouter une étape…", "Aucune option trouvée").
- **Controls are infinitives**: a button, menu item or tooltip that names an
  action is "Enregistrer", "Ajouter une étape", "Télécharger", never an
  imperative ("Enregistrez"). **Instructions are imperatives with vous**:
  "Ajoutez au moins un seuil", "Choisissez une autre cible". "Veuillez" is kept
  for requests to a participant or after an error ("Veuillez réessayer"), not
  for every instruction.
- **Product names take masculine agreement** (understood as "le logiciel"):
  "Interviewer a lui aussi été repensé", "Fresco… Hébergez-le".
- **Sentence case** for every title, heading, button and label: only the first
  word and proper names take a capital ("Bibliothèque de ressources", "Échec de
  la validation du protocole"). Never English title case. Capitals keep their
  accents: "Écran de fin", "État".
- **Interface names are proper names**: capitalised as the picker shows them,
  in « guillemets », after a common noun: "une étape « Sociogramme »",
  "l’interface « Narration »", "une consigne « Classement par catégories »".
  Never lowercase one and never turn it into an adjective or a plain noun:
  "l’ancien arbre généalogique" reads as somebody’s family tree, so write
  "l’ancienne interface « Arbre généalogique »". The exception is the two
  picker labels that begin with "Interface" (Anonymisation Interface,
  Geospatial Interface): write them as plain descriptions, "l’interface
  d’anonymisation", "une étape géospatiale".
- **Spaces before high punctuation**: put a no-break space (U+00A0) before
  ":", ";", "!" and "?", and inside guillemets: "« Autre »". Most catalog
  strings already use U+00A0 there (about 1,300 places, and no U+202F narrow
  no-break space anywhere), so use U+00A0 everywhere, including before ";",
  "!" and "?" where the Imprimerie nationale would prefer the narrow space. A
  plain space is wrong: the line can break before the punctuation mark. About
  600 places still have a plain space, most of them in the interview wording;
  fix them as their strings next change. No space before "," and ".", nor
  inside parentheses.
- **Quotation marks are « »** with U+00A0 inside. Never straight "…" quotes in French
  text; curly “…” only for a quotation inside a quotation. Text in code
  (`automaticLayout`, `{placeholder}`) keeps its own characters.
- **The apostrophe is typographic ’ (U+2019)**, as in every catalog (no
  straight `'` in French text anywhere).
- **The ellipsis is one character "…"** (U+2026), never three full stops, as
  in a placeholder "Rechercher…".
- **"e-mail"**: "adresse e-mail", "envoyer par e-mail", as the apps and the
  website write it ("courriel" does not appear in any catalog).
- **Numbers and dates**: decimal comma ("2,5"), a no-break space between
  thousands ("12 000") and before a unit or "%" ("10 Mo", "50 %"); dates as
  "2 septembre 2026", with no capital on the month.
- **Abbreviations**: "p. ex." in tight UI text, "par exemple" in prose.
- **Words**: "télécharger" for download. For bringing a protocol or file into
  Architect or Interviewer, write "importer" (Interviewer’s home screen says
  « Importer un protocole »; a resource is "ajoutée" or "importée" in
  Architect), whatever verb the English uses: those apps keep protocols,
  resources and interview data in the browser or on the device, so never say
  they upload them to a server. (Architect’s automated protocol review is the one exception: it sends the
  protocol to the review service. Both apps also send anonymous usage and
  error statistics, which never contain protocols, resources or interview
  data; in Interviewer they are on by default and can be turned off in its settings, Architect has no setting to turn them off, and neither is opt-in.) Fresco is a server, and its
  interface uses both verbs for adding a protocol (« Importer des
  protocoles », « Téléverser un protocole », « Téléversez et gérez vos
  protocoles d’entretien »): in Fresco prose follow the English verb,
  "téléverser" for upload and "importer" for import, and quote the label when
  naming a control. "téléverser" is of Québec origin (France’s official
  term, FranceTerme, says "télécharger" for both directions); it is kept
  because Fresco ships it. Gloss it on a page’s first use: "téléverser
  (envoyer vers le serveur)". Website and Architect strings that say
  "téléverser" for Architect or Interviewer are being changed. "Internet" takes a capital. "Cloud" is
  kept ("dans le cloud").
- **Screenshots stay in English.** Where a page names what a screenshot shows,
  give the French label the app shows.
- **Schema numbers**: "le schéma 9", "les protocoles en schéma 8", "la
  version du schéma", lowercase in prose. The documentation names schema 9;
  the website says "le schéma le plus récent" without a number.
- On a term’s first use on a documentation page, gloss the specialist terms
  marked _Gloss_ below.

## Never translated

Network Canvas, Architect, Interviewer, Fresco, Studio, Classic (Architect
Classic, Interviewer Classic), Server, file formats (`.netcanvas`, CSV,
GraphML, GeoJSON), third-party product names (Mapbox, UploadThing), and
anything inside code blocks or inline code. "Interviewer" the app is never
translated; the person who conducts an interview is "la personne qui mène
l’entretien".

## Inclusive language

Use wording that is neutral by construction: "la personne participante", "les
personnes participantes", "l’équipe de recherche", "les équipes de
recherche", "un membre de l’équipe de recherche", "la personne qui mène
l’entretien", "les personnes", "chaque alter". This is what the catalogs
already do (about 340 strings say "personne(s) participante(s)"; none says
"les participants"). Never use the point médian or other contracted forms
("participant·e·s", "chercheur.euse.s", "participant(e)s"): they lengthen the
text and screen readers read them badly. Avoid doubled forms ("chercheuses et
chercheurs", "le ou la participant·e") where a neutral construction says the
same; they lengthen the text without adding anything. The legal terms in the
data-protection table keep the masculine the law uses ("responsable du
traitement", "sous-traitant", "délégué à la protection des données").

# Arabic (ar) terminology glossary

How Network Canvas names its concepts and interfaces in Arabic. It applies to
every surface with Arabic text: the apps' and packages' catalogs, the wording
Network Canvas supplies inside protocols, networkcanvas.com and the
documentation. Translate with it, and review translations against it.

The Arabic is Modern Standard Arabic under the plain `ar` tag. It was written
without a native-speaking reviewer: every term here is a considered choice,
not a settled one, and a native speaker's correction replaces it. When a
translation needs a term that is not here, look for it in the catalogs first
(`packages/*/src/locales/ar.json`, `apps/*/src/locales/ar.json`), then add it
here in the same change. A new or changed entry is verified like any
translation (see the `verifying-translations` skill), and catalog strings that
use an old term change with it.

## Register and style

- **Modern Standard Arabic**, formal and plain. No dialect words.
- **Controls are verbal nouns** (المصدر): a button, menu item, tab or tooltip
  that names an action is written "حفظ", "إلغاء", "حذف", "إضافة سؤال", never
  an imperative ("احفظ") and never a first-person verb.
- **Statements are impersonal** where that reads naturally: "تعذّر حفظ
  التغييرات", "يجب إدخال اسم", "يمكن تغيير ذلك لاحقًا", "لم يُعثر على نتائج".
- **Instructions that must address the reader** use the masculine singular as
  the generic form: "اختر", "أدخل", "يمكنك". Never doubled forms ("اختر/ي",
  "المشارك/ة") and never the plural "أنتم" for one reader.
- **Text a participant reads does not address them with a gendered verb**
  where an impersonal sentence says the same: "يُرجى اختيار قيمة واحدة على
  الأقل" or "يجب إدخال اسم" in place of "اختر" and "أدخل". The masculine
  imperative is kept for researchers, and for a participant only where the
  impersonal form would be unclear.
- **People are named in the masculine as the generic form**: "المشارك",
  "الباحث", "القائم بالمقابلة". Prefer a plural or a construction with no
  gender where one is as short and as clear ("المشاركون", "أحد الوالدين").
- **Digits are Western** (0–9), as the `ar` tag formats numbers. Never
  Arabic-Indic digits (٠–٩) in a message.
- **Punctuation is Arabic**: "،" for a comma, "؛" for a semicolon, "؟" for a
  question mark. The full stop, colon, exclamation mark and the ellipsis "…"
  are shared. Keep an English message's closing punctuation: a label with no
  full stop has none in Arabic, a placeholder that ends in "…" ends in "…".
- **Quotation marks are « »**, with no space inside: «أخرى». Use them where
  the English quotes a label or a value.
- **Diacritics only where a word would be misread without one**: the tanween
  on an adverb ("لاحقًا", "تلقائيًا"), a shadda that separates two words
  ("مولِّد" is written "مولّد"), the vowels that mark a passive verb ("يُحفظ",
  "تُسجَّل") and the last vowel of a short imperative ("ألغِ", "أعطِ"). Prefer a
  wording that needs none ("استخدم في التصفية" for "صفِّ"). Never full
  vocalisation.
- **Never name a physical side** ("على اليسار", "أقصى يمين الشاشة") where the
  English says left or right of something on screen: the Arabic layout is
  mirrored. Say "بجانب", "في بداية السطر" or "في نهاية الشاشة".
- **A value filled into a sentence** (`{name}`, `{count}`) is not quoted. Do
  not attach a one-letter word to it: write "إلى {name}" or "مع {name}" in
  place of "لـ{name}" or "بـ{name}" wherever the sentence allows, and
  "لـ {name}" with a tatweel and a space where it does not. "و" is the
  exception and is written against the value ("و{name}").
- **A filled-in value has no grammatical case and no known gender.** Build the
  sentence so nothing has to agree with it: "العقدة {name}" (the noun carries
  the agreement), not an adjective or a verb that depends on what the value
  turns out to be.
- **Latin text inside Arabic** (a product name, a file format, a code value)
  keeps its own spelling and is not transliterated. A one-letter word before
  it takes a tatweel and a space: "لـ Fresco", "بـ CSV".
- **A number the message formats itself** (`{days, number}` beside a noun, with
  no `plural`) cannot change the noun's form. Write the number, then "من" and
  the definite plural, which is grammatical for every number: "آخر
  {days, number} من الأيام". Never turn `{n, number}` into a `plural`: the
  catalog guard requires the argument as the English has it.
- **Plurals have six forms.** Every `plural` gives `zero`, `one`, `two`, `few`,
  `many` and `other`, with the noun in the form the number requires:
  `zero {لا توجد عقد}`, `one {عقدة واحدة}`, `two {عقدتان}`, `few {# عقد}`
  (3–10, plural), `many {# عقدة}` (11–99, singular accusative: "# عنصرًا"
  for a masculine noun), `other {# عقدة}` (100 and above, singular). The dual
  takes "ان" or "ين" by its place in the sentence.

- **Arrows point the way the text reads.** A menu path or a "go on" arrow is
  "←" in Arabic: «الإعدادات» ← «الخصوصية». The arrow characters are not
  mirrored by the layout, so the catalog has to carry the turned one.

## Never translated

Network Canvas, Architect, Interviewer, Fresco, Studio, Mapbox, GraphML, CSV,
GeoJSON, `.netcanvas`, and anything written as code (`minValue`, a file name,
a URL). "Interviewer" the app is never translated; the person who conducts an
interview is "القائم بالمقابلة".

## Gender

Arabic marks gender on nouns, adjectives and verbs, and has no neutral form.
The rule, by Josh's ruling of 11 November 2026: use wording that is neutral by
construction (verbal nouns for controls, impersonal sentences, plurals), and
where a sentence must name or address one person, use the masculine as the
generic form. A protocol's own text is the researcher's, who can use dynamic
text keyed on an ego attribute to choose between masculine and feminine
wording. Do not use doubled or slashed forms.

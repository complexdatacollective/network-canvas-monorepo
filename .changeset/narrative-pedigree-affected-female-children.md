Three changes to how the Narrative Pedigree handles an X-linked recessive condition.

When a stage shows possible statuses, the children of a woman recorded as affected are now marked: her daughters may carry the condition and her sons may develop it, and this continues down through her daughters. A child whose sex is uncertain is left unmarked. Before, her children were only marked when her own mother had been worked out to carry or possibly carry the condition, so the result depended on which other relatives had been recorded. On its own, her being affected adds nothing about her parents.

A woman who is not affected is now shown as carrying the condition when it appears on two sides of her. A side is the line of one of her children or her mother's family. Two affected sons are one example. Others pair an affected son, or an affected grandson through a daughter, with an affected mother, a mother who carries the condition, an affected brother who shares her mother, an affected sister who shares her mother and whose father is recorded, or an affected maternal uncle. Before, only two affected sons, or an affected son with an affected brother who shares her mother, had this effect. The sides count only when no one man could account for all of them through his daughters, so daughters by the same father give nothing on their own, and a daughter with no recorded father may be the daughter of any man recorded as the father of her mother's children. A female's line counts only when neither her father nor any man who may be her father is affected, and an affected female whose father is not recorded counts for nothing and ends her line. Both conditions apply to every female the line passes through, whether a daughter, a sister, an aunt or a niece. The women between her and those relatives are judged the same way, so with an affected maternal uncle her mother and grandmother are shown as carrying it too unless her father or her mother's father is affected, while the mother of two sisters by one father, or of one sister by a recorded father and one with no recorded father, is not marked on their lines alone. Only biological and donor links count.

When a woman who carries the condition is focused and her father is affected, the highlighted path now runs only through her father, even when her mother is also affected. If she is herself affected, the path still runs through both parents.
